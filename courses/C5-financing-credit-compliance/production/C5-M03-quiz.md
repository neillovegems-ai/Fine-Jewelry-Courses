# Module 3 Assessment: Deferred Interest, Promo Periods and Plain-Language Explanations

### 10-Question Comprehensive Examination with Rationales

#### Q1: What is the fundamental operational difference between "True 0% APR" and "Deferred Interest"?
- A) True 0% APR is only available for costume jewelry
- B) In True 0% APR, no interest accrues during the promo; in Deferred Interest, interest accrues in the background from day one and is retroactively charged if the balance is not paid in full by the deadline
- C) Deferred interest is illegal in the United States
- D) True 0% APR requires customers to pay in physical cash
*Correct Answer:* B
*Rationale:* In deferred interest financing, finance charges accrue behind the scenes at the standard APR and are only waived if the customer pays the principal down to $0.00 before the promotional expiration date.

#### Q2: If a customer finances a $5,000 diamond necklace on a 12-month deferred interest promotion at 29.99% APR and has an unpaid balance of $25.00 on the final expiration date, what happens?
- A) The bank forgives the $25.00
- B) The bank charges 29.99% interest only on the $25.00 for the following month
- C) The bank assesses full retroactive interest calculated on the average daily balance over the entire 12-month period, adding approximately $800–$900 in finance charges to the account
- D) The store must repossess the necklace
*Correct Answer:* C
*Rationale:* Under deferred interest terms, failing to satisfy the entire balance by the deadline forfeits the promotional waiver, triggering retroactive interest on the entire purchase from day one.

#### Q3: Why does paying only the "Minimum Monthly Payment" stated on a credit card statement lead to a deferred interest penalty?
- A) Minimum payments are illegal on promotional cards
- B) Statement minimum payments are calculated as low percentages (typically 2.5% to 3.5%), which are mathematically insufficient to pay off the principal balance within the promotional term
- C) The bank refuses to accept minimum payments
- D) Minimum payments only cover bank transaction fees
*Correct Answer:* B
*Rationale:* Credit card minimum payment formulas are designed to stretch debt over years, leaving a large unpaid balance at the end of a 6, 12, or 24-month promotional term.

#### Q4: What is the "N - 1 Rule" for calculating safe monthly payoff schedules?
- A) Subtracting 1% from the sales tax
- B) Dividing the total financed amount by the total promotional months minus one (e.g., dividing by 11 for a 12-month promo) to guarantee payoff a full month prior to expiration
- C) Taking one diamond out of the setting
- D) Giving the customer a 1-month grace period before their first payment
*Correct Answer:* B
*Rationale:* The N - 1 rule creates a 30-day cushion, ensuring the balance is completely satisfied before the promo window closes, protecting the client from payment clearing delays.

#### Q5: Under the Credit CARD Act of 2009, how must credit card issuers allocate payments in excess of the minimum during the last two billing cycles of a deferred interest promotion?
- A) They must apply the excess payments to balances with the lowest APR
- B) They must apply the excess payments entirely to the expiring deferred interest promotional balance
- C) They can keep the excess payment as a merchant service fee
- D) They must refund the excess payment to the consumer's checking account
*Correct Answer:* B
*Rationale:* 12 CFR § 1026.53 specifically mandates that excess payments during the final two billing cycles must be applied directly to the deferred interest balance to help consumers avoid retroactive penalties.

#### Q6: Which of the following advertising or counter statements violates CFPB compliance guidelines for deferred interest promotions?
- A) "No Interest if Paid in Full within 12 Months"
- B) "12 Months Free Money with No Interest Ever"
- C) "12 Months Promotional Financing with payments calculated to pay off before expiration"
- D) "Deferred interest financing available; ask associate for details"
*Correct Answer:* B
*Rationale:* Stating "Free Money with No Interest Ever" is deceptive and misleading because interest is accruing in the background and will be assessed if conditions are not met.

#### Q7: A customer purchases a $4,400 ring on a 12-month deferred interest plan. Using the N - 1 rule, what monthly payment should the associate recommend?
- A) $366.67
- B) $400.00 ($4,400 / 11 months)
- C) $110.00
- D) $880.00
*Correct Answer:* B
*Rationale:* $\$4,400 / (12 - 1) = \$4,400 / 11 = \$400.00$ per month. This clears the balance in month 11, ensuring complete safety.

#### Q8: What should an associate do with the calculated monthly payoff amount at the point of sale?
- A) Keep it a secret so the bank earns interest
- B) Write it down clearly on the customer's take-home paperwork or receipt and explain the safe payoff plan
- C) Tell the customer to figure it out when their bill arrives
- D) Only disclose it if the customer files a formal complaint
*Correct Answer:* B
*Rationale:* Writing the payoff amount on the receipt and explaining it clearly reinforces transparency, prevents surprises, and cements consumer trust.

#### Q9: What typical standard APR is assessed on retail private-label jewelry cards if a customer defaults on a deferred interest promo?
- A) 0.0% to 5.0%
- B) 10.0% to 15.0%
- C) 28.99% to 34.99% Variable
- D) 75.0% Fixed
*Correct Answer:* C
*Rationale:* Major retail private-label credit card issuers currently set standard variable purchase APRs in the 28.99% to 34.99% range.

#### Q10: Why do luxury customers appreciate associates who deliver transparent deferred interest disclosures?
- A) Because customers love complicated legal jargon
- B) Because transparent explanations demonstrate professional honesty, protect them from surprise fees, and show that the store values their long-term financial wellbeing
- C) Because it allows them to negotiate lower diamond prices
- D) Because it delays the time they have to spend in the store
*Correct Answer:* B
*Rationale:* Honest disclosures differentiate a reputable luxury guild jeweler from high-pressure commercial operations, building lasting customer loyalty.
