# Module 6 Production Outline: FTC Guides and Product Representation for Sellers

## 1. Executive Summary & Terminal Outcome
- **Terminal Outcome:** The fine jewelry retail professional applies the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) Jewelry Guides (16 CFR Part 23) and Jewelers Vigilance Committee (JVC) compliance standards to every counter sales conversation, mastering mandatory diamond origin disclosures, gemstone treatment revelations, precious metal stamping fineness tolerances, and compliant appraisal presentations.
- **Key Operational Competencies:**
  - Applying Section 23.25 diamond nomenclature: distinguishing earth-mined diamonds, laboratory-grown diamonds, and simulants with statutorily approved descriptors.
  - Executing Section 23.22 gemstone treatment disclosures based on permanence, special care, and value impact.
  - Navigating National Stamping Act and FTC standards for gold (10k minimum), platinum alloys (950 vs 500 plat), sterling silver, and vermeil.
  - Eliminating deceptive pricing and inflated counter appraisal claims ("appraises for double") under FTC deceptive advertising guides.
  - Conducting receipt and invoice auditing to guarantee statutory compliance on all executed transactions.

## 2. Core Narrative Architecture
1. **The Law of the Showcase:** Understanding 16 CFR Part 23 (The FTC Jewelry Guides) and the enforcement role of the FTC, state Attorneys General, and the Jewelers Vigilance Committee (JVC).
2. **Diamond Nomenclature & Origin Disclosures (§ 23.25):**
   - The default legal rule: The word "Diamond" standing alone without qualification legally means an un-treated diamond formed in the earth.
   - Laboratory-Grown Diamonds: Clear and conspicuous disclosures; mandatory use of *"laboratory-grown," "laboratory-created,"* or *"[Manufacturer]-created."* Strict prohibition of misleading descriptors (*"natural," "real," "semi-precious"*).
   - Simulants (CZ, Moissanite): Must never be represented as diamonds; mandatory simulant disclosure.
3. **Gemstone Treatment Disclosures (§ 23.22):**
   - The Three-Part Disclosure Test: (1) Is the treatment non-permanent? (2) Does it require special cleaning/care? (3) Does it significantly impact value?
   - Specific protocols for glass-filled rubies, irradiated blue diamonds, heated sapphires, and oiled emeralds.
4. **Precious Metal Marking & Tolerance Laws (§§ 23.3–23.8):**
   - Gold: 10K statutory minimum in the US; fineness tolerances (3 parts per thousand without solder; 7 parts with solder).
   - Platinum: Minimum alloy standards (950 Plat vs 900 vs 500 Plat two-part alloy marks).
   - Silver & Vermeil: 92.5% pure silver; vermeil requirements (sterling base + 10k+ gold + 2.5 microns minimum thickness).
5. **Appraisal and Deceptive Pricing Prohibitions:**
   - Why telling a customer *"This piece appraises for $10,000, so you're making $4,000 in instant equity"* is illegal deceptive advertising.
   - The legal distinction between replacement cost for insurance and fair market cash value.
6. **The Counter Compliance Checklist:** A step-by-step invoice verification routine.
