# Course C4: Store Security, Loss Prevention & High-Value Transaction Protocols
## Module 6: Cards, Checks, Wires and Remote-Order Fraud (Pedagogical Blueprint)

- **Track:** Core Foundations &bull; Operations & Compliance Track
- **Personas:** P1 (New Sales Associate), P3 (Cruise/Travel Specialist), P7 (Store Leader / Boutique Director)
- **Word Target:** ~3,500–4,500 words (No-cap policy)
- **Research Anchor:** Reference R7 & R8 (Fine-Jewelry-Retail-Career-and-Skills-Reference.md Section 3.3: Cards, checks, wires and remote orders); JCK/JSA: "Protect Yourself From Credit Card Fraud and Bad Checks"; Jewelers Mutual 24/7 Security Protocols; NAJ SecurityWise eCommerce Fraud Guide.

### Pedagogical Objectives
1. **The Non-Cash Fraud Threat Spectrum:** Contrast physical loss prevention with non-cash financial fraud. Explain why credit card chargebacks, counterfeit cashier's checks, and remote-order scams cause higher aggregate financial write-offs in fine jewelry than physical theft.
2. **Card-Present Floor Discipline & The EMV Liability Shift:**
   - How the EMV liability shift operates: If a stolen chip card is swiped via magnetic stripe or key-entered manually without EMV insertion, merchant liability shifts 100% to the retailer, and Jewelers Mutual policies do NOT cover the loss.
   - Matching physical photo ID to the credit card name; checking for tactile card tampering (re-embossed numbers, shaved hologram stickers).
   - Obtaining and archiving physical signatures and terminal authorization receipts.
3. **The Cleared-Funds Mandate for Checks and Bank Drafts:**
   - The fatal "Bank Confirmation" trap: Why calling a bank to verify that funds exist is dangerous (a confederate can drain the account 10 minutes later, or the check itself may be drawn on a stolen commercial account).
   - The absolute rule: Merchandise is never released until the check has fully cleared and settled into the merchant's operating bank account (typically 3 to 5 business days).
4. **Remote-Order, Phone, and eCommerce Fraud Red Flags:**
   - Deconstructing the anatomy of a fraudulent remote order: The unsolicited phone/email inquiry, lack of price negotiation, urgent overnight delivery demand, shipping address differing from billing address, and subsequent "re-route" requests via carrier.
   - Layered verification stack: AVS (Address Verification Service), CVV2 matching, 3D Secure / Verified by Visa, reverse phone directory lookup, and requiring direct bank wire for first-time remote buyers.
5. **Bank Wire Verification Architecture:**
   - Why domestic and international wire transfers are the gold standard for high-ticket transactions over $25,000.
   - Verifying incoming wire settlement with the store's banking institution before releasing goods (avoiding fraudulent ACH transfers masquerading as wires).
