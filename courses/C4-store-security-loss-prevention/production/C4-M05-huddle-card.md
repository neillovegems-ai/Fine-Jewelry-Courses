# Daily Huddle Card: Course C4: Module 5
## Cash Over the Threshold: Form 8300 Step by Step

### ⏱️ 5-Minute Morning Briefing for the Sales Floor

**Core Principle:** *Form 8300 is not a tax; it is federal law enforcement reporting to prevent money laundering. Compliance is non-negotiable, and client confidentiality is absolute.*

---

### 1. When Does Form 8300 Apply?
- **The Trigger:** Any receipt of **more than $10,000 in cash** (or cash equivalents like cashier's checks under $10,000) from a single customer within a 24-hour window, OR across related transactions over a 12-month period.
- **Example:** A customer buys a diamond ring for $8,000 cash today, and a $3,500 band next week. Total cash = $11,500. Form 8300 is triggered!

---

### 2. Required Customer Information at Point of Sale
When a customer pays with $10,000+ in cash, you MUST obtain and verify:
1. Full legal name and physical home address (no P.O. boxes).
2. Social Security Number (SSN) or Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN). If foreign, passport number and country of issuance.
3. Government-issued photo ID (driver's license or passport).
- **Script:** *"Federal anti-money laundering regulations require us to record standard identification for cash receipts over $10,000. May I please see your driver's license?"*

---

### 3. The Anti-Tipping-Off Rule (CRITICAL)
- If a customer attempts to "structure" payments (*"What if I pay $9,500 today and $9,500 tomorrow so you don't have to report it?"*), you must decline the structuring, document the event, and check the **Suspicious Transaction Box**.
- **The Cardinal Rule:** **NEVER tell the customer** you checked the suspicious box. Tipping off is a federal felony!
