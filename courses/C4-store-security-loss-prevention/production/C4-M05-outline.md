### Pedagogical Objectives
1. **The Legal Framework of Anti-Money Laundering (AML):** Explain why fine jewelry retail is classified under the USA PATRIOT Act and Bank Secrecy Act as a covered sector. Detail the statutory mandate of IRS/FinCEN Form 8300 (Report of Cash Payments Over $10,000 Received in a Trade or Business).
2. **What Counts as "Cash" Under IRS Rules:**
   - Physical currency (U.S. Federal Reserve notes and foreign coins/paper currency).
   - Cash equivalents: Cashier's checks, bank drafts, traveler's checks, and money orders with a face amount of **$10,000 or less**, if received in a "designated reporting transaction" (retail sale of consumer durables like jewelry).
   - What is NOT cash: Personal checks drawn on the payer's account, wire transfers, and credit card charges.
3. **The Aggregation Rule: Related Transactions:**
   - The 24-hour single-transaction rule.
   - The 12-month related-transaction rule: If a client pays $6,000 cash for a ring today, and returns 3 weeks later to pay $5,000 cash for a matching wedding band as part of the same bridal project, the $10,000 threshold is triggered and Form 8300 must be filed within 15 days of the second payment.
4. **Mandatory E-Filing & Filing Timelines:**
   - Mandatory electronic filing via the BSA E-Filing System (effective January 1, 2024 for businesses filing 10+ information returns).
   - The strict **15-calendar-day** filing deadline.
5. **The Annual Payer Statement Rule:**
   - Mandatory written statement furnished to the customer on or before **January 31** of the following year.
   - Permitted format (an itemized sales receipt/invoice containing required statutory disclosure language).
   - Why sending a copy of the actual Form 8300 itself is non-compliant and discouraged due to sensitive SSN/TIN disclosures.
6. **Structuring, Red Flags, and the Anti-Tipping-Off Mandate:**
   - What is "Structuring" (criminal smurfing): Breaking payments into sub-$10,000 amounts specifically to evade reporting.
   - Checking the "Suspicious Transaction" box on Form 8300.
   - **The Absolute Federal Rule:** NEVER disclose or tip off the customer that a suspicious Form 8300 has been filed. Tipping off is a federal felony carrying severe criminal penalties.
