### Pedagogical Objectives
1. **The Psychology of Distraction Theft:** Deconstruct how professional distraction rings exploit natural human instincts (courtesy, eagerness to please, greed, sensory confusion). Explain the four distinct specialized roles within a professional distraction team:
   - *The Anchor / Big Spender:* Captivates the primary associate with an extravagant custom or high-carat request, flashing large cash or black cards.
   - *The Disrupter:* Enters moments later, creating sensory noise (spilling water, demanding immediate battery replacement, crying children, or language barriers).
   - *The Mechanic / Thief:* Moves into the blind spot created by the disrupter, sliding open unlocked case doors or palming open tray goods.
   - *The Mule:* Receives the stolen goods outside or takes the merchandise immediately out the door so that if the team is stopped, no stolen goods are found on the shoppers.
2. **Anatomy of the Sleight-of-Hand Switch:**
   - How counterfeit CZ and moissanite replicas are engineered to match exact millimeter dimensions.
   - Classic palming techniques (the coin palm, the finger palm, the drop-and-swap, the double-ring tray grab).
   - Why immediate post-presentation 10x louping neutralizes the switch before the thief exits the counter.
3. **Grab-and-Run Countermeasures & Spatial Architecture:**
   - The anatomy of the grab-and-run runner (pacing near exits, asking to view pieces outside of the counter, testing door lock responsiveness).
   - Mirror placement rules: Never place full-length mirrors adjacent to entrance doors; mirror viewings must be positioned inward toward the back salon.
   - Buzz-in door lock interlocks: Operating buzz-in doors safely without trapping armed criminals.
4. **Immediate Floor Responses to Suspicious Group Encounters:**
   - The "All Hands on the Floor" protocol (using the store code word to deploy colleagues to shadow every visitor).
   - Securing open cases instantly upon unannounced group entry.
