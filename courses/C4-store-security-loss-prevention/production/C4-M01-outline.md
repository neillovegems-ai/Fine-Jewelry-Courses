# Course C4: Store Security, Loss Prevention & High-Value Transaction Protocols
## Module 1: Why Jewelry Is Targeted: The Threat Landscape (Pedagogical Blueprint)

- **Track:** Core Foundations &bull; Operations & Compliance Track
- **Personas:** P1 (New Sales Associate), P3 (Cruise/Travel Specialist), P7 (Store Leader / Boutique Director)
- **Word Target:** ~3,500–4,500 words (No-cap policy)
- **Research Anchor:** Reference R7 & R8 (Fine-Jewelry-Retail-Career-and-Skills-Reference.md Section 3.1–3.3); Jewelers' Security Alliance (JSA) Annual Crime Reports; Jewelers Mutual Safety & Security Academy; National Jeweler Crime Analytics.

### Pedagogical Objectives
1. **Physical & Economic Drivers:** Explain the four intrinsic properties that make fine jewelry a prime target for organized criminal syndicates (extreme value-to-weight ratio, immediate liquid divisibility, untraceability once stones are dismounted/recut, international fungibility).
2. **The Modern Attack Spectrum:** Deconstruct the empirical hierarchy of retail jewelry crime according to JSA data:
   - Smash-and-Grab raids (sledgehammers, multi-suspect timing, 60–90 second clearance windows).
   - Distraction Theft & Sleight-of-Hand (multi-confederate teams, simulated chaos, cash baiting).
   - Grab-and-Run encounters (countertop posture exploits, opportunistic runner tactics).
   - Rooftop and Wall-Breach Off-Hours Burglaries (circumventing perimeter sensors, safe attacks).
   - Armed Robbery (violent coercion, showcase clearing, hostage dynamics).
3. **Retail Salon Vulnerability Scoring:** Teach associates and store leaders how to audit their own sales floor topography (sightlines from the street, display case layout, camera blind spots, showcase glass ratings, access chokepoints).
4. **Behavioral Situational Awareness:** Differentiate between normal client shopping behaviors and pre-operational surveillance cues (loitering without merchandise focus, observing camera angles, noting associate shifts, testing door chimes).
