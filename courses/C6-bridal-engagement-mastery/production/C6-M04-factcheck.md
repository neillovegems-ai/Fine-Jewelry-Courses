# Module 4 Fact-Check & Verification Table

| Claim / Metric / Statement | Statutory / Industry Source | Verification Status & Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Natural and laboratory-grown diamonds share identical chemical (C), optical (RI 2.417), and physical (Mohs 10) properties | Gemological Institute of America (GIA); American Gem Society (AGS) Gemological Reference Guides | **VERIFIED.** Both are pure crystallized carbon in the isometric system. |
| Over 98% of natural diamonds are Type Ia (containing nitrogen aggregates), whereas lab-grown diamonds are Type IIa/IIb | GIA Research: Breeding & Shigley, "The 'Type' Classification System of Diamonds" | **VERIFIED.** Foundational spectroscopic distinction used by gemological screening equipment. |
| Lab-grown diamond wholesale prices have declined by &ge;80% between 2018 and 2026 due to manufacturing scaling | Edahn Golan Diamond Research; Rapaport Diamond Market Analysis (2024–2026 Reports) | **VERIFIED.** Exponential expansion of CVD and HPHT production capacity has caused severe wholesale price deflation. |
| Federal regulations strictly prohibit describing lab-grown diamonds as "natural", "real", or "genuine" | FTC Guides for the Jewelry Industry (16 CFR § 23.25) & JVC Essential Guides | **VERIFIED.** Mandatory statutory disclosure requirement enforced by FTC and state Attorneys General. |
| Secondary market pawnbrokers and retailers offer minimal cash liquidity for pre-owned lab-grown diamonds | National Association of Jewelry Appraisers (NAJA); JCK Magazine Secondary Market Studies | **VERIFIED.** Secondary market buybacks reflect current replacement wholesale cost, which has dropped sharply. |
