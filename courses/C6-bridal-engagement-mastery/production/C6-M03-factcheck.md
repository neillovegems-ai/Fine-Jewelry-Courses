# Module 3 Fact-Check & Verification Table

| Claim / Metric / Statement | Statutory / Industry Source | Verification Status & Notes |
|---|---|---|
| A 2.0-carat oval diamond has approximately 9% to 12% more exposed face-up area than a 2.0-carat round brilliant | Gemological Institute of America (GIA) Diamond Proportions Database; Stuller Millimeter Guides | **VERIFIED.** Ovals carry less weight in depth and have greater length spread, yielding higher surface area per carat. |
| Classic length-to-width ratio for oval brilliant diamonds is 1.35:1 to 1.45:1 | GIA Gems & Gemology Fancy Cut Proportions; American Gem Society (AGS) Standards | **VERIFIED.** Standard gemological aesthetic benchmark for balanced oval silhouettes. |
| Step cuts (emerald, Asscher) reveal inclusions more easily due to open rectangular tables | GIA Diamond Clarity Grading Criteria; Diamonds and Diamond Grading (GIA Courseware) | **VERIFIED.** Step cuts lack multiple brilliant facets to break up light; VS2+ clarity is standard recommendation. |
| Platinum 950 displaces metal under abrasive wear rather than losing microscopic mass | Johnson Matthey Precious Metals Technical Handbook; Santa Fe Symposium on Jewelry Manufacturing | **VERIFIED.** Platinum's high ductility causes furrowing/patina rather than metal loss, preserving prong thickness over decades. |
| Cathedral setting arches distribute lateral impact forces across the shank perimeter | Stuller Bench Jeweler Technical Manual; GIA Jewelry Design & Technology | **VERIFIED.** Structural triangular geometry of cathedrals significantly reduces prong shear failure compared to peg heads. |
